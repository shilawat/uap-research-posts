# Ogden, 1949: A Silver Object Falls Near an Army Depot — The FBI and Air Force File the Same Report on the Same Day

On April 5, 1949, a guard at the Army General Supply Depot in Ogden, Utah observed what appeared to be an explosion in the air, followed by a silver-colored object falling at high altitude west of Ogden near Taylor, Utah. **Both Project Blue Book and the FBI filed records of this event on the same day, from the same location, independently — one of the tightest cross-agency corroborations in the entire UAP Knowledge Graph database**.

The spatial-temporal match: 4.33 km apart, 0 days separation.

---

## The Event

The witness — a guard at a US Army supply installation — reported:

- An apparent explosion in the air at high altitude
- A silver-colored object observed falling after the flash
- Location: west of Ogden, near Taylor, Utah
- Time: daytime observation by a trained military security guard

The description is consistent with multiple 1949-era reports of falling or descending objects — a period of intense government concern about the possibility of Soviet aerial vehicles or weapons systems operating over US territory.

---

## Why Ogden Matters

The Army General Supply Depot at Ogden was not a routine installation. During the late 1940s, Ogden's depot complex was part of the US military's critical logistics and supply infrastructure during the early Cold War buildup. The proximity of an unexplained aerial event — specifically one involving a descending silver object following an apparent explosion — to a strategic supply depot was exactly the kind of incident that triggered parallel government responses.

The Air Force's interest was aerial: what was this object, where did it come from, was it a threat to US airspace?

The FBI's interest was counterintelligence and ground security: was this a foreign device? Did anything land? Was the depot at risk?

Both agencies investigated. Neither found an explanation. Neither referenced the other.

## The 1949 Context

April 1949 sits at a critical juncture. The Soviet Union would test its first nuclear device just five months later, in August 1949. US intelligence was acutely aware of Soviet aviation and missile development programs. Any unidentified aerial event near a strategic installation in early 1949 was filtered through this lens.

The Ogden event has never been explained. It does not appear in any public joint Air Force-FBI assessment. The two records have sat in separate archives — the National Archives (Blue Book) and the FBI Vault — for 76 years. The UAP Knowledge Graph has placed them in the same database for the first time.

## A Pattern in 1949

The Ogden event is not isolated. 1949 was one of the most active years for dual-agency UAP documentation in the Knowledge Graph. Multiple Blue Book ↔ FBI matches cluster in this period, suggesting a systematic pattern of parallel Air Force and FBI investigation that has not previously been recognized or reported.

This pattern — two agencies, same events, separate files, no cross-reference — appears to have been standard operating procedure rather than an exception.
